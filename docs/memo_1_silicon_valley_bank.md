# Credit committee memo 1: Silicon Valley Bank (cert 24735), as at 30 September 2022 (ILLUSTRATIVE)

Information date: 30 September 2022 (Q3 2022). Only information available at that date is used in the analysis. The failure in March 2023 is described in a separate section at the end and is not used in the main analysis. Financial figures are in thousands of US dollars unless stated. Exposures are illustrative.

## Recommendation

**Hold. Do not approve a new or increased limit at this date.** Keep the illustrative exposure at zero until the points in Key risks are checked against the Q3 2022 filing.

At 30 September 2022 the headline ratios looked reasonable: equity to assets was 7.2% and loans to deposits was 40%. But the data I have does not show how much of the securities portfolio had lost value, or how concentrated the deposits were. Those two points decide the credit question, and they are not in the warehouse.

## Key risks

1. **Unrealised securities losses.** Rising interest rates in 2022 reduce the market value of long-dated bonds. If the losses are large compared with equity, the bank's capital is weaker than the equity ratio suggests. The Q3 2022 filing needs checking for this before any decision.
2. **Deposit concentration.** A bank with a concentrated or uninsured deposit base can lose funding quickly. The warehouse does not show deposit size or insurance status.
3. **Thin capital margin.** Equity to assets of 7.2% leaves a modest buffer if losses come through.

## Mitigants

- Loans to deposits of 40% means deposits cover loans about 2.5 times over, on the figures shown.
- Equity was $15.1bn at September 2022, up from $14.6bn in March. It peaked at $15.4bn in June.

## Monitoring triggers

Re-open this recommendation if any of the following happens:

1. The Q3 2022 filing shows unrealised securities losses greater than 50% of total equity. This is an analyst threshold, chosen for this memo.
2. Equity to assets falls below 6%. This is an analyst threshold, not a regulatory one.
3. Loans to deposits rises above 100%. This matches the Module D early-warning rule, which has not passed its gate.

## Conclusion

Hold. Approve no new limit at 30 September 2022. The headline ratios look reasonable, but the securities portfolio and the deposit base are not in the data and decide the credit question. Check the Q3 2022 filing before any limit is approved.

## What happened and what the analysis missed

The main analysis above used only information available at 30 September 2022. In March 2023, Silicon Valley Bank failed after a fast run on its deposits. Public reports at the time said that most of its deposits were uninsured, and that large unrealised losses on its held-to-maturity securities had reduced the value of its capital.

Looking back, this memo missed the two points the failure turned on: the securities losses and the deposit concentration. Both are outside the warehouse data. The hold recommendation was right for the wrong reason. Its ratios looked healthy, so a reviewer could have approved a limit without checking the securities portfolio. The lesson is that a ratio table is not enough for a bank with a large securities book in a rising-rate year.
