Data as of 30 June 2026 (latest quarter in the warehouse). Memo date: 25 November 2026. Financial figures are in thousands of US dollars unless stated. Exposures are illustrative and do not describe a real trading relationship.

## Recommendation

**Monitor. Keep the illustrative exposure at its current level and do not increase it.**

The bank's capital and earnings have improved over the last two years. Its base internal grade is 5, but a credit shock moves it to grade 10. Four triggers would change this recommendation (see Monitoring triggers).

## Internal rating

- Base grade: 5 (on a 1 to 10 scale, where 10 is the worst).
- Base probability of default: 0.05%, which is the model's floor.
- The expert score comparison is not in the data I used, so I cannot say whether the scorecard and expert views agree.

## Key risks

1. **Loan concentration against deposits.** Loans to deposits is 90.4% and has been close to 90% for two years. A deposit outflow would push the ratio towards 100%.
2. **Credit shock sensitivity.** Under the credit shock scenario the grade moves from 5 to 10, and the probability of default rises from 0.05% to about 6.7%. This is a stress result, not a forecast.
3. **Missing asset quality data.** Without non-performing loans and charge-offs, the main credit risk for a bank cannot be checked. This is the largest limit on this memo.

## Mitigants

- Equity to assets of 12.9% is a cushion, and it has risen for eight quarters.
- Earnings have improved every quarter in the table.
- Illustrative collateral and netting are not applied to this exposure in the Module C data used here.

## Monitoring triggers

Review this recommendation if any one of these happens:

1. The internal grade reaches 8 or worse.
2. Loans to deposits rises above 100%.
3. Equity to assets falls below 10%.
4. The annualised return on assets falls below 0.5%.

Trigger 1 uses the Module D early-warning level, which has not passed its gate. Triggers 2 to 4 are analyst thresholds, chosen for this memo. They are not fitted to data.

## Conclusion

Monitor Texas Bank and Trust Company at the current illustrative exposure, with no increase. The bank has strengthened its capital and earnings over eight quarters. The main risks are a deposit outflow and the credit shock result. The memo cannot assess asset quality because that data is missing, so this recommendation should be re-checked once it is added.
